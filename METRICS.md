# Metrics

## North Star Metric

**Audits completed per week**

Why: An audit completed means a user got value from the product.
Everything else — leads captured, Credex consultations booked,
credits purchased — flows from this single action. If weekly audits
are growing, the product is working. If they plateau, something in
the funnel is broken.

DAU is wrong for this product because founders use it once a quarter,
not daily. Revenue is too far downstream to optimize at this stage.

## 3 Input Metrics

**1. Form completion rate**
(Audits completed / Form page visits)
If people land on the page but don't finish the form, the UX is
too complex or the value proposition isn't clear enough on arrival.
Target: >60% of visitors who add at least one tool should complete.

**2. Share rate**
(Audits shared / Audits completed)
The viral loop depends on people sharing their results. If nobody
clicks Share, the product won't grow organically.
Target: >15% of completed audits shared within 24 hours.

**3. Lead capture rate**
(Emails captured / Audits completed)
This is the direct business metric for Credex. If users complete
the audit but don't save their report, the tool isn't generating
leads.
Target: >25% of completed audits result in email capture.

## What to instrument first

1. Page view → tool added → audit submitted funnel (where do people drop?)
2. Which tools are most commonly added (product insight)
3. Average monthly savings surfaced per audit (health of audit logic)
4. High-savings flag rate (what % of audits show >$500 savings)
5. Credex CTA click rate among high-savings audits

## Pivot trigger

If after 500 completed audits the lead capture rate is below 10%,
the value shown in the audit is not compelling enough to justify
sharing contact details. At that point, either the audit logic needs
to surface bigger savings, or the lead capture ask needs to change
(earlier, later, or with a stronger incentive like a PDF report).
